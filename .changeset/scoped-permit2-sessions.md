Scope Permit2-route sessions like settlement-scoped ones. A session whose `crossChainPermits` entry names `ACROSS` (or `SAME_CHAIN` beside it), or omits `settlementLayers`, no longer gets the wildcard fallback action, and `access.kind` is `'scoped'`. Sessions built with an earlier version change permissionId and enable digest, so they must be enabled again.

- A scoped session needs a bound on what its claim may carry: `oneTimeUse`, or the permit's new `preClaimOps: 'none'`, under which the claim may not carry pre-claim calls. Without either (or `fallback`) it is refused with `PERMIT2_ROUTE_NEEDS_BOUND`. A `preClaimOps: 'none'` session settles only intents that need no pre-claim call (the account already holds the Permit2 allowance, no fee is taken from the `from` token, no native token is wrapped), and must be enabled before its first intent.
- The session may call `approve(Permit2, amount)` on each `from` token on its chain, capped at the largest `maxAmount` of that token's legs (uncapped if any leg sets none). The cap bounds what the session approves, not an allowance the account already holds. A declared approve on that token is refused with `PERMIT2_APPROVE_CONFLICT`.
- A leg in the native or wrapped native token on the session's chain adds the wrapped native `deposit()` and `withdraw(uint256)`, capped at the wrapped native `from` leg's `maxAmount`. A native `from` token is refused with `NATIVE_SOURCE_UNSUPPORTED`; name the wrapped native token.
- `allowFees` now applies to these permits too, adding the fee-collector transfer and paymaster calls. It needs the fee addresses `sdk.createSession` reads from `GET /chains` and a `from` token the orchestrator serves as a USD stablecoin. Without it, an intent that carries an app fee or a protocol fee taken from the `from` token does not settle.
- A permit needs a `from` token on the session's chain (`PERMIT2_ROUTE_NEEDS_FROM`) unless the session sets `fallback`, and even then when it sets `allowFees`.
- A scoped permit must admit `ACROSS` (`PERMIT2_ROUTE_NEEDS_ACROSS`): a smart account settles same-chain intents through the IntentExecutor. Omitting `settlementLayers` now admits only the `ACROSS` arbiters. A permit naming the retired Permit2 `ECO` arbiter is refused with `RETIRED_PERMIT2_LAYER`, with or without `fallback`; use `ECO_IE`.
- Intents signed with a scoped session are pinned to `settlementLayers: { include: ['ACROSS'] }` and to the permit's `from` tokens as source assets; an explicit `settlementLayers` or `sourceAssets` can only narrow them.
- `restrictToActions` or `swap` can now be combined with such a permit when it is bounded.
- Add `fallback: 'intentExecution'` to keep the previous wildcard action (the calls Rhinestone's intent-execution policy admits, with the permit's spending limit), or `fallback: 'sudo'` for a wildcard that may call any contract, any function, with any arguments and native value. Either one reports `access.kind: 'open'` and keeps the previous intent filter. `fallback` on a session without a Permit2-layer permit, or with `restrictToActions` or `swap`, is refused with `FALLBACK_NOT_APPLICABLE`.
- `ALLOW_FEES_ONLY_INTENT_EXECUTOR` is no longer raised. Sessions holding raw `claimPolicies` are unchanged.
